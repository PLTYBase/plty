The story 🦆

In 1799, when the first platypus specimen reached Europe, scientists refused to believe it. They inspected it for stitches, convinced someone had sewn a duck's bill onto a beaver as a prank. The platypus was literally the first animal in history to be accused of being fake. It wasn't. It was just built different: a mammal that lays eggs, produces venom, sweats milk, has no stomach, senses electricity with its bill, and glows blue-green under UV light. Nature shipped it anyway — no roadmap, no whitepaper, no permission.

Kamohashi (カモノハシ) is Japanese for "duck-bill" — the platypus. We took the most gloriously improbable animal on Earth and gave it the coin it deserves, on the chain it belongs on: Base. (The platypus loves bridges. Base is a bridge. It was meant to be.) No promises of riches. No fake utility. Just the world's weirdest mammal, launched the most transparent way we knew how.

Certified weird facts

Every one of these is real. The coin is the least weird thing on this page.

🥚 Eggs + milk

One of only five mammals on Earth that lay eggs — then it feeds its babies milk it sweats through its skin. No nipples. Nature improvised.

☠️ Actually venomous

Males carry venomous spurs on their hind legs that can drop a dog. Cute, cuddly-looking, and armed. Do not FUD the platypus.

⚡ Electroreception

Underwater it closes its eyes, ears and nose — and hunts by sensing electric fields with 40,000 sensors in its bill. It literally feels the current.

✨ Glows under UV

Platypus fur is biofluorescent — it glows blue-green under ultraviolet light. Scientists still aren't sure why. We assume it's for the vibes.

🍽️ No stomach

Food goes straight from gullet to gut. No stomach acid, no stomach at all. Every degen's portfolio can relate.

🐣 Puggles

Baby platypuses are called puggles. A group of platypuses is a paddle. Holders of $PLTY are, therefore, the paddle.

FAQ — honest answers only

The platypus was accused of being fake once. We're not repeating history.

Is $PLTY an investment?
No. $PLTY is a meme coin — a community, a joke, a party about the world's weirdest animal. It has no revenue, no product, and no promise of going up. Buy it the way you'd buy a funny t-shirt: because it makes you smile, with money you can fully afford to lose.
Can the team rug?
The things that make rugs possible are locked behind a 2-of-3 Safe multisig: contract ownership, upgrade rights, and the LP tokens. No single person — including the founder — can move liquidity, mint, or upgrade alone. Honest caveat: the LP is held by the multisig, not burned, so "2 of 3 signers" is the trust boundary. Everything is on-chain, so you can watch it live.
Why is the contract upgradeable and mintable?
We chose flexibility over theater, and we say it out loud instead of hiding it. Both powers sit behind the multisig and every use would be public on-chain. If the community prefers it frozen, ownership can be renounced — permanently disabling upgrades and minting. Until then: verify, don't trust.
Who holds the supply?
90% sits in the Aerodrome liquidity pool. 10% is a reserve held by the multisig for community stuff (contests, liquidity top-ups) — every movement is publicly visible. No hidden team wallets, no presale, no insiders. Check the holder list on Basescan any time.
Why Base?
Fees are cents, the chain is fast, the culture is fun, and it's where a new generation of on-chain weirdos lives. Also: the platypus is famous for loving bridges, and Base is literally a bridge to Ethereum. The lore wrote itself.
What's the roadmap?
There isn't one — on purpose. No fake milestones, no "CEX listing soon", no promises we can't keep. The plan is simple: keep being the weirdest, most transparent little coin on Base, make good memes, and grow the paddle one weirdo at a time. 🦆
Who is behind this?
A small independent team that thinks the platypus deserved a coin done properly. We stay pseudonymous — like most of crypto — which is exactly why everything here is designed so you don't have to trust us: verified source, multisig custody, public wallets, open repo.
